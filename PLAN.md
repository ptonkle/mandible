# Plan

Stage 2, written by the agent from `BRIEF.md`. `B2 Require step-by-step justification`.

The brief approves this plan in advance (autonomous mode), so building starts straight after it is written. Every game word follows `GLOSSARY.md`.

## Shape of the build

| Layer | Holds | Talks to |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `crates/core` | Every rule: map generation, simulation, pathfinding, fog of war, AI, lockstep protocol, menus and settings validation, action grid layout, tooltip content, camera, and the draw list for each frame. | Nothing. Values in, values out. |
| `crates/shim` | One `#[wasm_bindgen]` struct, `Game`, wrapping the core's `App`. Each method is one call. Floats from the browser (mouse, timestamps) are cast to integers here; that cast is the only translation. | The page |
| `web/` | Canvas drawing of the core's draw list, DOM for menus and the HUD, input forwarding, `localStorage`, and the WebRTC transport (`web/online.js`). No rules and no numbers. | The shim; peers only after Host or Join |
| `data/` | All on-screen text (`strings.json`) and the term hints (`glossary.json`). | Fetched by the page, checked by the core |

## Decisions in build order

| # | Decision | Reason | Test that shows it holds |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Integer world.** Positions are fixed-point (1 cell = 256 sub-units; a cell is about one ant wide). The simulation runs at 10 ticks per second; rendering interpolates between ticks. Game Speed changes milliseconds per tick (Slow 140, Normal 100, Fast 70). | D2 bans floats. Integers make every peer compute the same world, which lockstep needs. 10 Hz is plenty for a macro game and leaves time for 1,200 ants. | `two_worlds_with_the_same_seed_and_commands_stay_identical` (10,000 ticks, checksum each tick) |
| 2 | **Seeded randomness in the core:** one PCG32 generator inside the world, seeded from the match seed. | D2 bans `rand`. One generator in the world keeps randomness part of the state. | the same determinism test, plus `different_seeds_give_different_maps` |
| 3 | **Map generation.** Build one sector of chambers joined by tunnels (minimum width from Match Settings, never below 10 cells), then copy it by 180° rotation (2 seats) or 90° rotation (4 seats). Each home chamber gets a Crumb Pile, a Flower and a Leaf Litter nearby. Dirt pockets hold caches. Biome changes colours and decorations only. | Rotational symmetry makes maps fair by construction, and generation stays fast (one sector, then copies). | `every_home_chamber_has_all_three_nodes`; `home_chambers_are_equally_far_from_the_centre`; `every_open_cell_is_reachable`; `narrowest_tunnel_is_at_least_the_setting`; `generation_takes_under_50ms` (release) |
| 4 | **Navigation by flow fields.** One distance field per target (a waypoint, node, queen or blueprint) per colony, built with a bucket-queue Dijkstra over the cell grid and cached. A colony's own walls are passable to it; enemy walls, rock and dirt are not. Flying Ants use fields that ignore walls. A wall or dirt change invalidates the cache. | Per-ant A\* for 1,200 ants would be too slow. Fields share one search among every ant heading to the same place. | `ants_reach_a_waypoint_around_rock`; `enemy_walls_block_but_own_walls_do_not`; `flying_ants_cross_walls_not_rock`; `digging_dirt_opens_a_path` |
| 5 | **Crowding:** a spatial bucket grid. Ants push apart when they overlap more than 30% of a body width. | Keeps fights readable (Overlap) at low cost. | `ants_on_one_cell_spread_out`; `overlap_never_exceeds_the_limit_after_settling` |
| 6 | **Economy.** Harvester Ants pick nodes by Gather Focus and nearness and stop at saturation (8 per node). They fill their inventory, then deliver to the nearest queen. Leafcutter Ants gather Leaves only. Nodes deplete. Delivery adds to the stockpile at once. Income is a 10-second running average of deliveries, worked out in the core. | The brief's macro economy, with no micro. A running average shows what is really coming in. | `a_harvester_fills_and_delivers`; `saturation_sends_the_ninth_harvester_elsewhere`; `a_depleted_node_is_left`; `income_matches_deliveries_over_ten_seconds` |
| 7 | **Gather Focus** (new glossary term): Balanced (Food 2 : Nectar 1 : Leaves 1), or Food, Nectar or Leaves focus (3 : 1 : 1 toward that resource). | The brief wants gatherers automatic. Something still has to pick which resource, and a single global choice is macro, not micro. | `focus_changes_the_split_of_new_assignments` |
| 8 | **Building.** Placing a blueprint takes its cost from the stockpile at once. Carpenter Ants pick up the cost at the nearest queen and carry it in inventory-sized trips; when all of it is delivered they build it. Walls are lines up to 12 cells, placed by click and drag. Queens need a chamber. Structures other than walls do not block movement. | The brief requires resources to be carried to the blueprint. Taking the cost at placement stops two blueprints spending the same Food. | `a_blueprint_is_built_only_after_its_cost_is_delivered`; `cancelling_a_blueprint_refunds_its_cost`; `a_wall_blocks_enemy_paths_when_complete` |
| 9 | **Production.** Every queen and hatchery has one lane. Train, Upgrade and Evolve go to the least-busy eligible lane. Cost is paid when queued. Train 10 queues up to 10 that can be paid for; Train Max queues as many as can be paid for. Housing Full pauses lanes. Cancel refunds. | Global actions with no selection, as the brief asks, and the queue bar can show every lane. | `train_ten_queues_ten_or_what_is_affordable`; `train_max_spends_down_to_the_cheapest_remainder`; `housing_full_pauses_training`; `cancel_refunds` |
| 10 | **Species, structures, tiers and upgrades** as tables in `crates/core/src/rules.rs`. There are 12 species, from Harvester Ant to Dinosaur Ant. Upgrades are colony-wide: Damage, Armor and Health (I–III) and Gather Rate (I–III), Carry Capacity (I–II) and Build Rate (I). Each combat species has one species upgrade (I–II), such as Acid Ant Range or Fire Ant Move Speed. Rank II needs Tier 2 and rank III needs Tier 3. | Balance lives beside the rules it feeds. One species upgrade each keeps the Upgrades tab within 12 buttons. | `every_species_has_a_name_role_tier_and_cost`; `ranks_need_their_tier`; `armor_is_flat_with_a_minimum_of_one` (12 damage against 8 armor deals 4; 3 against 8 deals 1) |
| 11 | **Combat.** Ants attack the nearest enemy (ant, structure or blueprint) within their aggro radius every few ticks, staggered by ant id. Ranged attacks need a clear line through rock, dirt and walls. Splash hits every enemy within its radius. Exploding Ants die when they attack. Nurse Ants heal the most-hurt ally in range. Combat ants dig dirt that blocks their path. | Everything happens automatically, as macro play needs. | one test per behaviour, e.g. `splash_hits_every_enemy_in_radius`; `an_exploding_ant_dies_when_it_attacks`; `nurse_heals_the_most_hurt` |
| 12 | **Waypoints.** Each colony has up to 4 waypoints per species, plus an "All Ants" button that sets one for every combat species. Ants split evenly by stable assignment. Moving to a waypoint is attack-move. Retreat sends every ant except Gatherers and Builders to the nearest nest; switching it off sends them back. A species with no waypoint waits at the nearest nest. | Matches the brief and the decisions table. | `three_waypoints_split_ants_evenly`; `clearing_a_waypoint_reassigns_its_ants`; `retreat_overrides_waypoints_and_releases` |
| 13 | **Fog of war:** Bresenham rays from each occupied cell (deduplicated) to the vision radius, stopped by rock, dirt and walls, recomputed every 2 ticks for every colony. Allies share vision. Each cell remembers whether it has been explored. | Line of sight as the brief asks. Every colony is computed because the AI uses its own colony's vision. | `vision_does_not_turn_corners`; `allies_share_vision`; `explored_stays_explored` |
| 14 | **Events:** a Windfall or a Hill every 60–90 seconds from 1:30. A Hill lasts 45 seconds; the colony holding it longest gets a resource bundle. Every peer rolls them with the world's generator. | Keeps players moving, as the brief asks, and stays deterministic. | `events_are_identical_for_the_same_seed`; `holding_the_hill_longest_pays_the_holder` |
| 15 | **Victory and Defeat.** A colony is defeated when it has no ants, queens or hatcheries left, or when it concedes. A team wins when no enemy team remains. | The person's decision. | `walls_and_brood_cells_do_not_keep_a_colony_alive`; `conceding_defeats_at_once`; `last_team_standing_wins` |
| 16 | **AI inside the simulation.** Every peer runs it, so it sends nothing over the network, and it issues the same commands a player does. Larva: slow decisions, one nest, no upgrades. Pupa: a standard build, one expansion, upgrades. Imago: a fast economy, counters to what it has seen, multiple waypoints and a second expansion. A colony whose player drops is handed to Pupa on the same tick. | Deterministic AI needs no network traffic and never depends on the host. | `imago_beats_pupa_and_pupa_beats_larva` (10 seeded headless matches each, at least 7 wins, release profile); `a_dropped_colony_becomes_ai` |
| 17 | **Lockstep** (`crates/core/src/lockstep.rs`). Each player sends one turn per tick to every peer over a full mesh. A tick runs when every seat's turn has arrived. Each peer picks its own input delay from measured round-trip times (2–6 ticks) and can change it mid-match, because turns are per player. Every 20 ticks, a checksum of a compact digest rides on a turn. On a mismatch the coordinator (the lowest live seat) sends a snapshot and everyone resyncs. Dropping a player is agreed through the coordinator, so every peer drops on the same tick. If the host leaves, the next seat coordinates. | Changes from floodline and vagrancy (SECOND-ORDER rows 7–8): a mesh removes the host hop and the single point of failure; per-player turns let the delay adapt with no agreement; resync replaces "the match ends". | a `Loopback` transport with latency, jitter and loss. Tests include `four_peers_stay_in_step_for_5000_ticks`, `a_desync_is_resynced`, `a_dropped_peer_is_dropped_on_the_same_tick_everywhere`, `the_host_leaving_does_not_end_the_match`, `delay_changes_mid_match_keep_peers_in_step` |
| 18 | **Transport** (`web/online.js`): WebRTC data channels, one reliable ordered channel per peer. Signalling uses Trystero (Nostr strategy), vendored at a pinned version with a sha256 check, and imported only after Host or Join. A pasted-code path needs no relay; the automated two-tab test uses it with `?ice=none`. The core sends turns as JSON. | The person chose option (a). Only page code may touch the network, and only after a click. | `tests/browser/online.py`: two tabs, pasted codes, one complete short match, checksums equal |
| 19 | **Builder rule changes for online play,** each recorded in BUILDER-SPEC.md's table: (i) D4: `connect-src` adds the pinned Nostr relay URLs, which are needed only after Host or Join; (ii) the page scanner exempts `web/online.js` and `web/vendor/` from the WebRTC and URL bans only, while numbers and arithmetic stay banned in `online.js`; (iii) `build.sh` copies `web/vendor/`. R7 (nothing off-origin on load and idle) is kept and still checked in three engines. | The person approved the online path. Each exemption is as narrow as it can be and is named in the test itself. | the existing boundary tests, plus `only_online_js_may_name_webrtc` |
| 20 | **Drawing.** The core writes a flat `Int32Array` draw list each frame, in screen pixels: op, then arguments. Op codes, colours and ant shapes (13 species × 16 headings, as polygons) come from the core once at load. The map and fog are core-made RGBA pixel buffers at one pixel per cell, scaled by `drawImage`. Health pies are polygons made by the core. | D3 bans numbers in the page, so the page can only replay what the core lays out. Pixel buffers keep 65,000 cells cheap. | the boundary tests; `draw_list_names_only_known_ops`; the browser check draws a match without errors |
| 21 | **Interface.** Action grid tabs on keys 1–5 (Ants, Build, Waypoints, Upgrades, Colony). Buttons on `Q W E R / A S D F / Z X C V`, drawn with keyboard stagger. Ctrl gives Train 10 and Shift gives Train Max. Every hotkey can be remapped in Keybinds. Tooltips are label–value pairs built by the core. Term hints come from `data/glossary.json`. The resource bar shows income. The queue bar shows icons. There is a minimap. | The brief's interface, the glossary rules, and "no prose". | `hotkeys_follow_the_keyboard_shape`; `remapped_keys_fire_their_action`; `every_tooltip_is_label_value_pairs`; `every_hint_term_is_in_the_glossary` |
| 22 | **Menus.** The Main Menu offers Play vs AI, Online Multiplayer, Match Settings and Player Settings. Buttons are leaves, and choosing one sends an ant to drag its leaf away (CSS animation). The lobby uses the same match settings form. Default Match Settings and Keybinds are saved in `localStorage` and validated by the core on load. | The brief's menus. Browser storage is the simplest place for personal settings, and it never leaves the device. | `settings_out_of_range_are_rejected_by_name`; browser interactions in `tests/browser/interactions.py` |
| 23 | **Glossary guard.** A test reads `GLOSSARY.md` and `data/`. Every species, structure, upgrade and action name in the data must be a glossary term, and on-screen strings must not contain plain never-say words. | Makes "consistent language" checkable, not just a promise. | `on_screen_names_are_glossary_terms`; `strings_avoid_never_say_words` |
| 24 | **Performance.** 1,200 ants: a simulation tick under 8 ms in release native and the draw list under 4 ms, measured by a release-only test and recorded in MEASUREMENTS.md. `tool_core` gets `opt-level = 3` in the dev and test profiles. | The brief names performance as important. 8 ms of a 100 ms tick leaves room for wasm overhead and slower machines. | `twelve_hundred_ants_tick_within_budget` (release); a frame-time check in the browser |
| 25 | **Benchmark** (requested 2026-10-10). A Player Settings button starts the performance-test scenario live in the page: four colonies of about 300 ants each marching to the centre and fighting. A label–value panel shows frames per second, average and worst simulation tick, ant count, and the budget (tick under 8 ms, 60 frames per second). | So the person can see, not just read, what "acceptable performance" means. | browser interaction starts the Benchmark and reads the panel |
